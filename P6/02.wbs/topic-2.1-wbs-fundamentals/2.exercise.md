### Question 1: The "Folder vs. File" Paradigm & Mathematical Scheduling Contradictions
A junior full-stack developer joining your scheduling software engineering team proposes the following database refactor:
> *"Why maintain two separate database tables for `wbs_nodes` and `activities`? In our UI, they both appear in the same tree grid. Let's merge them into a single `tasks` table with a `parent_id` column. If a task has children, it behaves as a summary task; if it has no children, it's a leaf task. Users can add durations, resources, and Finish-to-Start dependencies directly to any parent task."*

1. Explain the architectural and mathematical reasons why commercial enterprise scheduling engines (e.g., Primavera P6) maintain a strict separation between WBS summary containers ("Folders") and CPM execution activities ("Files").
2. Demonstrate with a concrete directed acyclic graph (DAG) example how allowing dependencies directly between parent summary nodes and child activities can introduce circular dependency deadlocks into the Critical Path Method (CPM) forward/backward pass calculation engine.
3. If a summary node were permitted to have both its own direct planned duration/cost and rolled-up child metrics, what mathematical anomalies occur during portfolio-level Earned Value ($EV$) rollups?
