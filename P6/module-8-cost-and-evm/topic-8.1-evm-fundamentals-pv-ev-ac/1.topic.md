## 1. Executive Summary & The Core Mental Model

### 1.1 The "False Savings" Trap (The Burn-Rate Delusion)
In traditional corporate accounting and amateur project management, financial health is measured using two numbers: **Planned Spend** (Budget Cashflow) and **Actual Spend** (General Ledger Cash Outflow). 

Consider a typical status meeting for a ₹400 Crore infrastructure asset:
* **Planned Cashflow at Month 6:** ₹100 Crore
* **Actual Invoices Paid at Month 6:** ₹75 Crore
* **Finance Director's Verdict:** *"We have spent ₹25 Crore less than forecasted! We are under budget, and the project is highly cost-efficient!"*

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                   THE "FALSE SAVINGS" TRAP                                       │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                                                  │
│  TRADITIONAL ACCOUNTING VIEW (Flawed 2-Dimensional Comparison):                                 │
│                                                                                                  │
│  Planned Spend (Budget Cashflow) ────────► ₹100 Cr                                               │
│  Actual Spend (Invoices Paid)    ────────► ₹75 Cr                                                │
│                                            ───────                                               │
│  Apparent "Savings"              ────────► +₹25 Cr  (Management cheers: "Under budget!")        │
│                                                                                                  │
│  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────  │
│                                                                                                  │
│  THE PHYSICAL REALITY ON SITE (Enter Earned Value):                                              │
│                                                                                                  │
│  Budgeted Value of Work DONE     ────────► ₹50 Cr   (Only 12.5% of the plant was built!)         │
│                                                                                                  │
│  * To deliver ₹50 Cr worth of physical work, the contractor spent ₹75 Cr in actual cash!         │
│  * True Cost Overrun             ────────► -₹25 Cr  (Burning ₹1.50 to produce ₹1.00 of asset!)   │
│  * True Schedule Delay           ────────► -₹50 Cr  (Half of planned work was not delivered!)    │
│                                                                                                  │
│  CONCLUSION: The project is NOT saving money. It is hemorrhaging cash while falling disastrously  │
│  behind schedule. Traditional accounting completely concealed this project disaster.            │
│                                                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The fatal blind spot of traditional financial reporting is that **cash spend has no inherent relationship with physical accomplishment**. Spending less money than budgeted can mean one of two things:
1. The engineering team found brilliant efficiencies and delivered all planned work at lower cost; or
2. The site was completely gridlocked, contractors failed to mobilize, steel was not procured, and nothing was built.

Without a third metric that quantifies **physical progress converted into contractual monetary value**, you cannot distinguish between an efficient project and a dying project.

### 1.2 The Plain-English Analogy: The Taxi Meter and Odometer
To understand Earned Value intuitively before diving into differential calculus or ANSI standards, consider hiring a taxi to travel 100 kilometers across the desert:

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                             THE TAXI METER & ODOMETER ANALOGY                                    │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                                                  │
│  1. The Planned Route (Planned Value - PV):                                                      │
│     * Total Journey: 100 km. Planned Rate: ₹10/km. Total Budget (BAC): ₹1,000.                  │
│     * Schedule: You planned to be at the 50 km mark by 12:00 PM.                                │
│     * At 12:00 PM, Planned Value (PV) = 50 km × ₹10/km = ₹500.                                  │
│                                                                                                  │
│  2. The Odometer on the Dashboard (Earned Value - EV):                                           │
│     * At 12:00 PM, you look at the physical odometer. The car has only driven 30 km!             │
│     * What is that 30 km physically worth at your agreed contract rate of ₹10/km?                │
│     * Earned Value (EV) = 30 km × ₹10/km = ₹300.                                                │
│                                                                                                  │
│  3. The Taxi Fare Meter (Actual Cost - AC):                                                      │
│     * You look at the fare meter ticking on the dashboard. The driver was stuck in heavy sand,   │
│       engine idling with air conditioning running. The meter displays ₹450.                     │
│     * Actual Cost (AC) = ₹450.                                                                  │
│                                                                                                  │
│  EVALUATION AT 12:00 PM:                                                                         │
│  * Schedule Status: Planned 50 km (₹500), but completed only 30 km (₹300). Behind schedule!      │
│  * Cost Status: Work done is worth ₹300, but meter charged ₹450. Over budget!                   │
│                                                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

In this analogy:
* **$PV$** is where the map said you should be by now, converted to budget currency.
* **$EV$** is how far the car actually drove, valued at the baseline contract rate.
* **$AC$** is the cash registered on the meter that you must pay out of pocket.

### 1.3 What is Earned Value Management? The ANSI/EIA-748 Standard
**Earned Value Management (EVM)** is a project controls methodology that integrates a project's **Scope Baseline** (Work Breakdown Structure), **Schedule Baseline** (Critical Path Method logic), and **Cost Baseline** (Budget allocations) into a unified performance measurement framework.

EVM originated in the 1960s within the United States Department of Defense (DoD) as the *Cost/Schedule Control Systems Criteria (C/SCSC)*. In 1998, it was codified as the international commercial standard **ANSI/EIA-748** (currently maintained as EIA-748-D). 

ANSI/EIA-748 defines **32 Guidelines** organized into five core functional categories:

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                ANSI/EIA-748 FIVE PILLARS (32 GUIDELINES)                         │
├───────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Functional Category               │ Key Governance Requirements                                  │
├───────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Organization (GL 1–5)          │ Define WBS down to Control Accounts (CA); assign single-point│
│                                   │ ownership to Control Account Managers (CAM); integrate OBS. │
├───────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. Planning, Scheduling, and      │ Integrate master schedule with work packages; establish the  │
│    Budgeting (GL 6–15)            │ Performance Measurement Baseline (PMB); define Work Packages│
│                                   │ and Planning Packages; assign objective credit rules.        │
├───────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. Accounting Considerations      │ Record direct costs in the General Ledger on an accrual      │
│    (GL 16–21)                     │ basis; map general ledger charges cleanly to Control Accounts│
│                                   │ without timing distortions.                                  │
├───────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 4. Analysis and Management        │ Calculate monthly cost and schedule variances ($CV, SV$);    │
│    Reports (GL 22–27)             │ compute indices ($CPI, SPI$); develop realistic Estimates at  │
│                                   │ Completion (EAC); document corrective action plans.         │
├───────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 5. Revisions and Data             │ Control changes to the Performance Measurement Baseline;     │
│    Maintenance (GL 28–32)         │ prohibit retroactive changes to historical actuals or EV;    │
│                                   │ prevent baseline tampering ("moving the goalposts").         │
└───────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

When building an enterprise scheduling and project controls platform in Golang, your database engine and calculation pipelines must strictly enforce these five pillars.

## 2. The Core Triad & Mathematical Foundations

At any specific point in time—designated in Primavera P6 as the **Data Date (DD)** or **Status Date**—EVM calculates project health using three foundational values, all expressed in the **same unit of currency** (e.g., INR ₹, USD $, EUR €).

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                              THE CORE EVM PERFORMANCE TRIAD                                      │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                                                  │
│                                ┌───────────────────────────┐                                     │
│                                │ Budget at Completion(BAC) │                                     │
│                                │ Total Approved PMB Budget │                                     │
│                                └─────────────┬─────────────┘                                     │
│                                              │                                                   │
│                 ┌────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────┐                      │
│                 ▼                            ▼                            ▼                      │
│     ┌───────────────────────┐    ┌───────────────────────┐    ┌───────────────────────┐          │
│     │  PLANNED VALUE (PV)   │    │   EARNED VALUE (EV)   │    │   ACTUAL COST (AC)    │          │
│     │        (BCWS)         │    │        (BCWP)         │    │        (ACWP)         │          │
│     ├───────────────────────┤    ├───────────────────────┤    ├───────────────────────┤          │
│     │ "What should have     │    │ "What was physically  │    │ "What was actually    │          │

│                 └──────────────┬─────────────┘                            │                      │

│                 │   Schedule Variance (SV)    │                           │                      │
│                 │        SV = EV - PV         │                           │                      │

```

### 2.1 Planned Value ($PV$ / BCWS)
* **Formal Acronym:** Budgeted Cost for Work Scheduled ($BCWS$).
* **Definition:** The authorized budget allocated to scheduled work expected to be performed up to the Data Date.
* **Calculation:**
  $$PV(t) = \sum_{i \in \text{Activities}} \text{BAC}_i \times \% \text{Planned}_i(t)$$
* **Behavior:**
  * At Project Start ($t = 0$): $PV = 0$.
  * At Contract Baseline Completion ($t = T_{\text{baseline}}$): $PV = BAC$.
  * In a CPM network, $PV$ is determined by spreading an activity's budgeted cost over its baseline dates (Early Dates or Late Dates) according to a predefined distribution curve (linear, front-loaded, back-loaded, bell-curve).

### 2.2 Earned Value ($EV$ / BCWP)
* **Formal Acronym:** Budgeted Cost for Work Performed ($BCWP$).
* **Definition:** The quantified measure of work physically performed up to the Data Date, expressed in terms of the budget allocated for that work.
* **Calculation:**
  $$EV(t) = \sum_{i \in \text{Activities}} \text{BAC}_i \times \% \text{Physical Complete}_i(t)$$
* **The Immutability Invariant of EV:**
  Notice that $EV$ does **not** care how much money was actually spent to execute the work! If an activity with a budget of ₹10,00,000 is 50% complete, its Earned Value is *strictly* ₹5,00,000, even if the contractor spent ₹2,00,000 or ₹20,00,000. $EV$ values the physical output solely at the frozen baseline price.

### 2.3 Actual Cost ($AC$ / ACWP)
* **Formal Acronym:** Actual Cost of Work Performed ($ACWP$).
* **Definition:** The total recognized cost incurred in executing the work completed up to the Data Date.
* **Accounting Requirement:** Must include direct labor, material, subcontracts, equipment, and recognized **accruals** (work completed on site for which invoices have been received or logged, even if cash has not yet left the bank account).
* **Scope Boundary:** $AC$ must cover the exact same scope of work that generated the $EV$. If $AC$ includes ₹5 Crore spent on materials stored on site that have not yet been installed (and thus have not earned value), the comparison between $EV$ and $AC$ becomes mathematically corrupted.

### 2.4 Budget at Completion ($BAC$)
* **Definition:** The total approved budget baseline for the entire project scope.
* **Formula:**
  $$BAC = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \text{Budget}_i = PV(T_{\text{end}})$$
* **Management Reserve Exclusion:** In ANSI/EIA-748, $BAC$ represents the **Performance Measurement Baseline (PMB)** only. It strictly **excludes Management Reserve (MR)**. Management Reserve is an unallocated contingency held by the Owner or Executive Sponsor for unforeseen out-of-scope risks; it is only added into $BAC$ through formal baseline change requests.

## 3. Rules of Credit: How Physical Work Converts to Earned Value

The most common operational vulnerability in an EVM system is subjective or dishonest reporting of `% Physical Complete`. If a site superintendent claims an inverter foundation is "90% complete" when all they have done is dig an excavation trench, the system reports fictitious Earned Value.

ANSI/EIA-748 mandates formal **Rules of Credit (Earned Value Measurement Techniques - EVMT)** to eliminate subjectivity:

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                         ANSI/EIA-748 EARNED VALUE MEASUREMENT TECHNIQUES                         │
├────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Technique          │ Rule Mechanics                     │ Industrial Application & Use Case      │
├────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. 0 / 100 Rule    │ 0% EV until 100% physically done;  │ Short tasks (duration ≤ 1 reporting    │
│                    │ 100% EV credited on finish.        │ period, e.g., 5-day soil test pit).   │
├────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. 50 / 50 Rule    │ 50% EV upon task start (day 1);    │ Short-to-medium tasks spanning across  │
│                    │ Remaining 50% EV upon 100% finish. │ two reporting cycles (e.g., 20 days).  │
├────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. Milestone       │ Predetermined fractional weights   │ Long, multi-month complex tasks        │
│    Weights         │ credited as objective QA/QC gates  │ (e.g., Inverter Foundation installation│
│                    │ are formally signed off.           │ broken into 5 audited sub-steps).      │
├────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 4. Units Complete  │ EV = (Units Installed / Total Plan)│ Repetitive civil/electrical production │
│                    │ × Budget.                          │ (e.g., 32,000 driven steel piles;      │
│                    │                                    │ 250,000 PV modules mounted).           │
├────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 5. Percent Complete│ Direct engineering assessment      │ General tasks where standard unit      │
│    (Physical)      │ backed by measurable deliverables. │ metrics do not easily apply.           │
├────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 6. Level of Effort │ EV is dynamically set equal to PV  │ Overhead and support roles with no     │
│    (LOE)           │ ($EV \equiv PV$ at all times).     │ measurable physical deliverable (e.g., │
│                    │ Never produces schedule variance!  │ Site Security, Project Controls PMO).  │
└────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### The Milestone Weights Rule in Action: Solar Inverter Foundation
On our running project, Activity `ACT-INV-101` ("Construct 3.125MVA Inverter Station Foundation") has an approved budget ($BAC$) of **₹20,00,000** and a scheduled duration of 25 working days.

Rather than allowing the contractor to guess progress, the contract establishes an audited **Milestone Credit Gate**:

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   INVERTER FOUNDATION MILESTONE CREDIT SCHEDULE (BAC: ₹20,00,000)                │
├──────┬──────────────────────────────────────────────┬──────────┬──────────────┬──────────────────┤
│ Gate │ Engineering Milestone Deliverable            │ Weight % │ Value (₹)    │ QA/QC Inspection │
├──────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────┼──────────┼──────────────┼──────────────────┤
│ M1   │ Earthwork Excavation & PCC Mud Mat Pour      │   15%    │  ₹3,00,000   │ Joint Survey Sign│
│ M2   │ Rebar Cage Binding & Earthing Grid Mesh      │   30%    │  ₹6,00,000   │ Rebar Pre-pour   │
│ M3   │ Conduit/Cable Duct Stub-ups & Formwork       │   15%    │  ₹3,00,000   │ MEP Line-up Check│
│ M4   │ M30 Structural Concrete Pour & Test Cubes    │   25%    │  ₹5,00,000   │ Concrete Batch Tk│
│ M5   │ 28-Day Curing, Form Stripping & Backfill     │   15%    │  ₹3,00,000   │ Compaction Test  │
├──────┴──────────────────────────────────────────────┴──────────┴──────────────┴──────────────────┤
│ TOTAL                                               │  100%    │ ₹20,00,000   │ Final Sign-off   │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

If M1, M2, and M3 are certified by the Owner's Engineer, the physical % complete is strictly:
$$\% \text{Physical} = 15\% + 30\% + 15\% = 60\%$$
$$\text{Earned Value } (EV) = ₹20,00,000 \times 60\% = ₹12,00,000$$

Even if the contractor poured half of the concrete for M4 on the afternoon of the status date, zero credit is granted for M4 until the complete pour and concrete test cube sampling are officially signed off. This prevents premature revenue and progress recognition.

## 5. Cost-Schedule Integration: The Control Account Architecture

In commercial software engineering, the single biggest design flaw in project controls tools is attempting to link General Ledger cost accounts directly to individual schedule activities.

A modern utility-scale solar project schedule contains **8,000 to 25,000 activities**. In contrast, an SAP S/4HANA or Oracle ERP financial system tracks purchase orders, labor timesheets, and subcontractor invoices against **50 to 150 Cost Centers / WBS Elements**.

If an engineer attempts a 1-to-1 mapping between SAP Cost Centers and P6 Activities:
* Field workers must enter timesheet hours against thousands of individual activity codes, leading to widespread misallocation and garbage data.
* Accounting teams reject invoices because line items do not correspond to contractual vendor purchase order numbers.

ANSI/EIA-748 solves this through the **Control Account (CA)** architecture:

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   THE CONTROL ACCOUNT (CA) INTERSECTION ARCHITECTURE                             │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                                                  │
│                 WORK BREAKDOWN STRUCTURE (WBS) ──► "What physical asset is built?"               │
│                                                                                                  │
│                              WBS.4.0: Inverter Stations (32 Skids)                               │
│                                              │                                                   │
│                                              ▼                                                   │
│                        ┌───────────────────────────────────────────┐                             │
│                        │       CONTROL ACCOUNT (CA-INV-001)        │                             │
│                        │  "Inverter Civil & Skid Installation"     │                             │
│                        │  Manager (CAM): Rajesh Kumar (Lead Civil) │                             │
│                        │  BAC: ₹24,00,00,000                       │                             │
│                        └─────────────────────┬─────────────────────┘                             │
│                                              │                                                   │
│                 ORGANIZATIONAL BREAKDOWN     │   SAP / ERP Cost Collector:                       │
│                 STRUCTURE (OBS):             │   Internal Order: IO-992104                       │
│                 Civil Construction Dept      │   WBS Element: IN-RAJ-SOL-INV-CIV                 │
│                                              │                                                   │
│                 ┌────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┐                       │

```

### Key Principles of Control Accounts:
1. **Single Point of Responsibility:** Every Control Account is assigned to exactly one **Control Account Manager (CAM)**.
2. **Two-Way Rollup:**
   * Downward: A Control Account contains one or more detailed CPM **Work Packages (WP)** and far-term **Planning Packages (PP)**.
   * Upward: Work Packages roll up to the Control Account to compute $PV$ and $EV$.
3. **ERP Financial Linkage:** General ledger actual expenses ($AC$) are posted to the **Control Account level**, not to individual CPM activities!
   * The scheduling engine calculates:
     $$PV_{\text{CA}} = \sum_{w \in \text{WP}} PV_w + \sum_{p \in \text{PP}} PV_p$$
     $$EV_{\text{CA}} = \sum_{w \in \text{WP}} EV_w$$
   * The ERP system imports:
     $$AC_{\text{CA}} = \text{Sum of ERP Invoices and Accruals for IO-992104}$$
   * Cost Variance and Performance Indices are strictly evaluated at the Control Account level.

## 7. Golang EVM Calculation Engine

Below is a production-grade calculation engine implemented in Go that:
1. Calculates $PV$, $EV$, and $AC$ across all ANSI/EIA-748 credit rules;
2. Aggregates metrics up from activities to work packages, control accounts, and project root;
3. Detects inconsistencies and enforces mathematical boundaries.

```go
// Package evm provides high-precision Earned Value Management calculations.
package evm

import (
	"errors"
	"fmt"
	"math"
	"time"
)

// EVMMeasurementRule represents ANSI/EIA-748 Earned Value Measurement Techniques.
type EVMMeasurementRule string

const (
	RuleZeroHundred      EVMMeasurementRule = "ZERO_HUNDRED"
	RuleFiftyFifty        EVMMeasurementRule = "FIFTY_FIFTY"
	RuleMilestoneWeights  EVMMeasurementRule = "MILESTONE_WEIGHTS"
	RuleUnitsComplete     EVMMeasurementRule = "UNITS_COMPLETE"
	RulePhysicalPercent   EVMMeasurementRule = "PHYSICAL_PERCENT"
	RuleLevelOfEffort     EVMMeasurementRule = "LEVEL_OF_EFFORT"
)

// MilestoneCredit represents an objective milestone inside a work package.
type MilestoneCredit struct {
	ID        string
	Weight    float64 // Fractional weight, e.g., 0.25 for 25%
	Completed bool
}

// Activity represents an individual CPM task.
type Activity struct {
	ID             string
	Code           string
	BAC            float64 // Budget at Completion (₹)
	BaselineStart  time.Time
	BaselineFinish time.Time
	ActualStart    *time.Time
	ActualFinish   *time.Time
	PhysicalPct    float64 // 0.0 to 1.0 (0% to 100%)
	UnitsPlanned   float64
	UnitsCompleted float64
	Rule           EVMMeasurementRule
	Milestones     []MilestoneCredit
}

// ControlAccount represents the fundamental ANSI/EIA-748 control element.
type ControlAccount struct {
	ID          string
	Code        string
	Title       string
	BAC         float64
	Activities  []*Activity
	ActualCosts float64 // Aggregated from ERP ledger up to Data Date
}

// EVMMetrics contains the computed triad values and variances.
type EVMMetrics struct {
	BAC float64 `json:"bac"`
	PV  float64 `json:"pv"`
	EV  float64 `json:"ev"`
	AC  float64 `json:"ac"`
}

// Engine encapsulates the EVM calculation logic.
type Engine struct {
	DataDate time.Time
}

// NewEngine creates a new EVM engine anchored at the given status Data Date.
func NewEngine(dataDate time.Time) *Engine {
	return &Engine{DataDate: dataDate}
}

// CalculateActivityPlannedValue computes PV by linearly spreading budget across baseline dates.
func (e *Engine) CalculateActivityPlannedValue(a *Activity) float64 {
	if a.BAC <= 0 {
		return 0.0
	}
	// Case 1: Status date is prior to baseline start -> No planned value yet
	if e.DataDate.Before(a.BaselineStart) {
		return 0.0
	}
	// Case 2: Status date is on or after baseline finish -> 100% of budget planned
	if !e.DataDate.Before(a.BaselineFinish) {
		return a.BAC
	}
	// Case 3: Status date falls within the baseline duration -> Linear spread
	totalDuration := a.BaselineFinish.Sub(a.BaselineStart).Seconds()
	if totalDuration <= 0 {
		return a.BAC
	}
	elapsedDuration := e.DataDate.Sub(a.BaselineStart).Seconds()
	fraction := elapsedDuration / totalDuration
	return math.Round(a.BAC*fraction*100) / 100
}

// CalculateActivityEarnedValue computes EV based on the assigned ANSI/EIA-748 rule.
func (e *Engine) CalculateActivityEarnedValue(a *Activity) (float64, error) {
	if a.BAC < 0 {
		return 0, errors.New("negative BAC is invalid")
	}

	switch a.Rule {
	case RuleZeroHundred:
		if a.ActualFinish != nil && !a.ActualFinish.After(e.DataDate) {
			return a.BAC, nil
		}
		return 0.0, nil

	case RuleFiftyFifty:
		if a.ActualFinish != nil && !a.ActualFinish.After(e.DataDate) {
			return a.BAC, nil
		}
		if a.ActualStart != nil && !a.ActualStart.After(e.DataDate) {
			return math.Round(a.BAC*0.50*100) / 100, nil
		}
		return 0.0, nil

	case RuleMilestoneWeights:
		totalEarnedFraction := 0.0
		for _, m := range a.Milestones {
			if m.Completed {
				totalEarnedFraction += m.Weight
			}
		}
		if totalEarnedFraction > 1.0 {
			totalEarnedFraction = 1.0
		}
		return math.Round(a.BAC*totalEarnedFraction*100) / 100, nil

	case RuleUnitsComplete:
		if a.UnitsPlanned <= 0 {
			return 0.0, errors.New("units planned must be > 0 for UnitsComplete rule")
		}
		ratio := a.UnitsCompleted / a.UnitsPlanned
		if ratio > 1.0 {
			ratio = 1.0 // Physical EV capped at 100% of baseline budget
		}
		return math.Round(a.BAC*ratio*100) / 100, nil

	case RulePhysicalPercent:
		clampedPct := math.Max(0.0, math.Min(1.0, a.PhysicalPct))
		return math.Round(a.BAC*clampedPct*100) / 100, nil

	case RuleLevelOfEffort:
		// LOE rule: EV is strictly equal to PV at all times
		return e.CalculateActivityPlannedValue(a), nil

	default:
		return 0, fmt.Errorf("unsupported EVM rule: %s", a.Rule)
	}
}

// EvaluateControlAccount aggregates all child tasks and merges with ERP actual costs.
func (e *Engine) EvaluateControlAccount(ca *ControlAccount) (*EVMMetrics, error) {
	metrics := &EVMMetrics{
		BAC: ca.BAC,
		AC:  ca.ActualCosts,
	}

	totalChildBAC := 0.0
	for _, act := range ca.Activities {
		totalChildBAC += act.BAC
		pv := e.CalculateActivityPlannedValue(act)
		ev, err := e.CalculateActivityEarnedValue(act)
		if err != nil {
			return nil, fmt.Errorf("activity %s error: %w", act.Code, err)
		}

		metrics.PV += pv
		metrics.EV += ev
	}

	// Verify that Child Activities BAC matches Control Account BAC (sanity check)
	if math.Abs(totalChildBAC-ca.BAC) > 1.0 {
		return nil, fmt.Errorf("BAC mismatch in CA %s: CA.BAC=%.2f, sum(Child.BAC)=%.2f",
			ca.Code, ca.BAC, totalChildBAC)
	}

	metrics.PV = math.Round(metrics.PV*100) / 100
	metrics.EV = math.Round(metrics.EV*100) / 100
	metrics.AC = math.Round(metrics.AC*100) / 100

	return metrics, nil
}
```

## 8. Real-World Engineering Pitfalls & Anti-Patterns

### 8.1 Pitfall 1: Level of Effort (LOE) Abuse
* **The Anti-Pattern:** A project manager assigns the `LEVEL_OF_EFFORT` credit rule to core design or procurement tasks because "it is too difficult to measure weekly deliverables."
* **Why it Breaks the Engine:** By mathematical definition, $EV_{\text{LOE}} \equiv PV_{\text{LOE}}$. An LOE task **can never produce schedule variance**. If an engineering team spends 6 months designing the substation layout and is completely behind schedule, marking the task as LOE masks the delay by artificially fabricating Earned Value simply because time elapsed.
* **The Rule:** ANSI/EIA-748 limits LOE tasks to purely unmeasurable support activities (Project Administration, Site Security, Secretarial). LOE work must **never exceed 10% to 15%** of total project budget.

### 8.2 Pitfall 2: Double-Counting Uninstalled Material ("Material on Site")
* **The Anti-Pattern:** In Month 4, Bharat Solar receives 100,000 PV modules at the Bhadla desert warehouse and immediately credits 30% Earned Value against the PV Installation Work Package.
* **The Disaster:** PV modules sitting in wooden crates inside a warehouse generate zero electrical power. If the module frames are defective or if monsoon flooding damages the cartons before mounting, the project has credited phantom progress.
* **The Best Practice:** Material On Site (MOS) can be recognized in ERP inventory accounts, but must **never earn value** in the scheduling baseline until the modules are physically fastened to the tracker structures and inspected.

### 8.3 Pitfall 3: The ERP Invoice Lag Distorting Actual Costs ($AC$)
* **The Anti-Pattern:** Subcontractors finish ₹10 Crore worth of civil grading in Month 3. The progress is verified, so $EV$ increases by ₹10 Crore. However, the subcontractor submits their invoice 60 days later, and the Owner's accounts payable department takes another 30 days to approve it.
* **The Result:** In Month 3, $EV = ₹10\text{Cr}$ but $AC = ₹0$. The system calculates an apparent cost savings of ₹10 Crore ($CV = +₹10\text{Cr}$). Two months later, when the ₹10 Crore invoice hits the ledger along with subsequent charges, $AC$ spikes to ₹20 Crore, causing the project's cost metrics to crash violently.
* **The Solution:** EVM actual cost engines must calculate **Accrued Costs** (Earned Value accompanied by an estimated liability journal entry for unbilled work) rather than relying solely on paid bank debits.
