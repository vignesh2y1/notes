# Topic 8.4: Forecasting: BAC, EAC, ETC, VAC, TCPI — Where Will We Finish Financially?

---

## 1. Executive Summary & The Core Mental Model

### 1.1 The Ultimate Boardroom Question: "How Much Will This Actually Cost?"
In enterprise project controls, historical data ($PV$, $EV$, $AC$) tells you where you have been and where you stand today. But executive committees, bank syndicates, and infrastructure investors do not fund projects to look in the rearview mirror. Their single, relentless question at every status review is:

> **"Given where we are today, how much more money must we disburse to finish, and what will the final damage be when we commission the plant?"**

This is the domain of **Earned Value Forecasting**.

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                 THE RETROSPECTIVE VS FORECASTING SPLIT                           │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ RETROSPECTIVE (Looking Backwards: What Happened)│ FORECASTING (Looking Forward: Where We End)    │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Planned Value (PV)  = ₹220 Crore              │ • Estimate at Completion (EAC) = ₹466.7 Crore  │
│ • Earned Value (EV)   = ₹180 Crore              │ • Estimate to Complete (ETC)   = ₹256.7 Crore  │
│ • Actual Cost (AC)    = ₹210 Crore              │ • Variance at Completion (VAC) = -₹66.7 Crore  │
│ • Cost Variance (CV)  = -₹30 Crore              │ • To-Complete Index (TCPI_BAC) = 1.158         │
│ • Schedule Var (SV)   = -₹40 Crore              │ • Status: Baseline Blown; Cash Call Required   │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Forecasting is **not wishful thinking**. It is the rigorous mathematical projection of future cash requirements based on past performance efficiency, known commercial realities, and remaining physical work.

### 1.2 The Plain-English Analogy: The Himalayan Expedition Rations
To understand the relationship between $BAC$, $EAC$, $ETC$, $VAC$, and $TCPI$ intuitively, imagine leading a high-altitude expedition to summit a 7,000-meter Himalayan peak:

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                             THE HIMALAYAN EXPEDITION ANALOGY                                     │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                                                  │
│  1. The Original Ration Plan (Budget at Completion - BAC):                                       │
│     * Total Expedition Budget: 1,000 kg of high-calorie ration packs.                            │
│     * Summit Plan: Reach Camp 4 (60% up the mountain) by Day 15, consuming 500 kg.              │
│                                                                                                  │
│  2. The Day 15 Base Camp Audit (Actual Cost vs Earned Value):                                    │
│     * Actual Consumption (AC): Your team battled brutal headwinds and consumed 600 kg.          │
│     * Physical Progress (EV): You are only at Camp 3 (40% up the mountain, worth 400 kg work).   │
│     * Cost Performance Index (CPI): EV / AC = 400 / 600 = 0.667.                                 │
│       (For every 1.0 kg of rations consumed, your team climbed only 0.667 kg worth of altitude!) │
│                                                                                                  │
│  3. The Forecasting Questions at Day 15:                                                         │
│     * Estimate to Complete (ETC): "How many more kilograms of rations do we need to reach       │
│       the summit from Camp 3?"                                                                   │
│     * Estimate at Completion (EAC): "What will the total food bill be when we touch the summit?" │
│     * Variance at Completion (VAC): "How much more food did we consume than the original 1,000kg?"│
│     * To-Complete Performance Index (TCPI): "If we refuse to order emergency helicopter drops   │
│       and insist on surviving on our original 1,000 kg budget, how fast must our metabolism      │
│       become on the frozen upper slopes?"                                                        │
│                                                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

In this expedition:
* If you assume the blizzard headwinds will continue all the way to the summit, your total ration burn will be $EAC = \frac{1000}{0.667} = 1,500\text{ kg}$. You will run out of food and perish at 6,200 meters unless an emergency helicopter resupply ($ETC = 900\text{ kg}$) is ordered.
* If your expedition doctor insists you can still summit on the remaining $400\text{ kg}$ ($1000 - 600$), your climbers would need to achieve a performance efficiency of $TCPI = \frac{1000 - 400}{1000 - 600} = \frac{600}{400} = 1.50$. In other words, they must climb **50% more efficiently than their original ideal plan**, in sub-zero oxygen-deprived death zones, after struggling at $0.667$ on the easy lower slopes. 
* As expedition leader, you know this is biologically impossible. The expedition must either secure more rations or turn back.

### 1.3 The Fundamental Triad of Forecasting
Every forecasting calculation balances three variables:

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                 THE TRIAD OF EVM FORECASTING                                     │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                                                  │
│                     [Past Sunk Reality]               [Remaining Work]                           │
│                       Actual Cost (AC)               (BAC - Earned Value)                        │
│                              │                                │                                  │
│                              └───────────────┬────────────────┘                                  │
│                                              ▼                                                   │
│                                  [Future Efficiency Rate]                                        │
│                                      Hypothesis (H_fut)                                          │
│                                              │                                                   │
│                                              ▼                                                   │
│                                  ESTIMATE AT COMPLETION                                          │
│                                    EAC = AC + ETC                                                │
│                                                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Past Sunk Reality ($AC$):** Cash already spent cannot be unspent. In accounting and law, sunk costs are fixed historical facts.
2. **Remaining Work ($BAC - EV$):** The contractual value of physical deliverables that have not yet been produced.
3. **Future Efficiency Hypothesis ($H_{\text{future}}$):** The critical engineering assumption: At what rate will the team convert dollars into physical work from this day forward?

Depending on your assumption for $H_{\text{future}}$, you arrive at fundamentally different forecasts.

## 2. Core Metrics: Definitions & Mathematical Axioms

Before deriving the predictive formulas, let us define the fundamental metrics and their units.

```text
┌────────┬─────────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Metric │ Formal Title                        │ Definition / Plain English Meaning                │
├────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ BAC    │ Budget at Completion                │ Total approved baseline cost of the project/scope.│
│ AC     │ Actual Cost                         │ Total actual cost incurred up to Data Date.       │
│ EV     │ Earned Value                        │ Budgeted cost of physical work performed to date. │
│ EAC    │ Estimate at Completion              │ Expected total cost of project upon completion.   │
│ ETC    │ Estimate to Complete                │ Expected cost required to finish remaining work.  │
│ VAC    │ Variance at Completion              │ Expected budget surplus (+) or overrun (-) at end.│
│ TCPI   │ To-Complete Performance Index       │ Cost efficiency required on remaining work to hit │
│        │                                     │ a specified target (BAC or EAC).                  │
└────────┴─────────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 2.1 The Axiomatic Relationship: $EAC = AC + ETC$
By definition, the total cost when the project is finished ($EAC$) must equal the money already spent ($AC$) plus the money yet to be spent ($ETC$):

$$EAC = AC + ETC$$

Equivalently, the Estimate to Complete is:

$$ETC = EAC - AC$$

### 2.2 The Commercial Verdict: $VAC = BAC - EAC$
The **Variance at Completion ($VAC$)** expresses the projected monetary gap between the approved contract baseline and the final anticipated cost:

$$VAC = BAC - EAC$$

* **If $VAC > 0$ (Positive):** The project is forecasted to finish **under budget** (Favorable / Cost Savings).
* **If $VAC = 0$ (Zero):** The project is forecasted to finish exactly on budget.
* **If $VAC < 0$ (Negative):** The project is forecasted to finish **over budget** (Unfavorable / Cost Overrun).

> **Crucial P6 & ANSI Standard Rule:**  
> Just like $CV = EV - AC$ and $SV = EV - PV$, **positive is always favorable** and **negative is always unfavorable**. If a developer writes $VAC = EAC - BAC$, an overrun of ₹50 Crore would show up as $+₹50\text{ Cr}$, violating international EVM convention.

## 3. The 4 Canonical EAC Formulas & When to Use Each

In enterprise project management (ANSI/EIA-748 and Primavera P6), there is no single "one-size-fits-all" EAC formula. A project controls specialist chooses the formula that models the **physical and commercial reality of the remaining scope**.

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                 THE FOUR CANONICAL EAC FORMULAS                                  │
├───────┬────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┤
│ Case  │ Governing Condition    │ EAC Formula                        │ Future Efficiency (CPI_fut)│
├───────┼────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────┤
│ 1     │ Typical Performance    │ EAC = BAC / CPI                    │ CPI_fut = CPI_past         │
│ 2     │ Atypical (One-off)     │ EAC = AC + (BAC - EV)              │ CPI_fut = 1.0 (on budget)  │
│ 3     │ Schedule Impact (CSI)  │ EAC = AC + [(BAC - EV)/(CPI*SPI)]  │ CPI_fut = CPI_past * SPI   │
│ 4     │ Bottom-Up Re-estimate  │ EAC = AC + Bottom-Up ETC           │ Re-estimated from scratch  │
└───────┴────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────┘
```

---

### 3.1 Formula 1: Typical Past Performance ($EAC = \frac{BAC}{CPI}$)

#### Mathematical Derivation:
Assume that past cost efficiency ($CPI$) is representative of all future work. That is:

$$CPI_{\text{future}} = CPI_{\text{past}} = \frac{EV}{AC}$$

The cost to complete the remaining work ($BAC - EV$) at this efficiency rate is:

$$ETC = \frac{\text{Remaining Work}}{CPI} = \frac{BAC - EV}{CPI}$$

Now, substitute $ETC$ into the fundamental equation $EAC = AC + ETC$:

$$EAC = AC + \frac{BAC - EV}{CPI} = AC + \frac{BAC}{CPI} - \frac{EV}{CPI}$$

Recall that $\frac{EV}{CPI} = \frac{EV}{EV / AC} = AC$. Therefore:

$$EAC = AC + \frac{BAC}{CPI} - AC = \frac{BAC}{CPI}$$

$$\mathbf{EAC = \frac{BAC}{CPI}}$$

#### When to Use:
1. **Systemic Inefficiencies:** Structural productivity problems that are unlikely to change (e.g., lower rock-drilling penetration rates than expected, higher local labour union rates, chronic supply chain friction).
2. **Steady-State Operations:** The project is in mid-execution (20% to 80% complete) and conditions on site are homogeneous.
3. **Default Statistical Baseline:** The standard benchmark formula mandated by the US Department of Defense and major audit agencies.

---

### 3.2 Formula 2: Atypical Past Variance ($EAC = AC + (BAC - EV)$)

#### Mathematical Derivation:
Assume that all variances accumulated to date were caused by **isolated, non-recurring external shocks** (force majeure, one-time engineering redesign, flash flood). The root cause has been fully resolved, and all remaining work will proceed **exactly as originally budgeted** ($CPI_{\text{future}} = 1.00$).

$$ETC = \frac{BAC - EV}{1.00} = BAC - EV$$

Substituting into $EAC = AC + ETC$:

$$\mathbf{EAC = AC + (BAC - EV)}$$

Notice that since $BAC - EV - AC = (BAC - AC) - EV$, we can also express this as:

$$EAC = BAC - (EV - AC) = BAC - CV$$

#### When to Use:
1. **Isolated Unforeseen Blip:** A single supplier went bankrupt and was replaced with an insured alternative; a 100-year rain event submerged the site for two weeks; a custom clearance strike halted port offloading for 10 days.
2. **Defensive Claim by Contractors:** Contractors frequently prefer this formula because it predicts the **smallest possible final cost overrun** ($VAC = CV$).
3. **CAUTION — Auditor Warning:** Project managers often fall into the psychological trap of declaring *every* bad variance "atypical." If a project has had a $CPI < 0.90$ for 6 consecutive months, claiming the variance is "atypical" is professional malpractice.

---

### 3.3 Formula 3: Schedule Delay Impacting Cost ($EAC = AC + \frac{BAC - EV}{CPI \times SPI}$)

#### The Compounding Physics of Schedule and Cost:
In civil and industrial engineering, a project that is behind schedule ($SPI < 1.0$) will almost invariably suffer **accelerating cost overruns**. Why?
1. **Time-Related Fixed Overheads (Standing Charges):** Project management teams, tower crane rentals, site security, diesel generators, and perimeter dewatering pumps burn cash every day the site remains open, regardless of physical progress.
2. **Acceleration & Crashing Costs:** To recover lost time, contractors pay overtime wages (1.5x to 2x rates), deploy night shifts, and expedite shipping by air freight rather than sea.
3. **Liquidated Damages (LDs):** Delay penalties clause in EPC contracts (e.g., 0.1% of contract value per day of delay).

To capture this compounding risk, we divide the remaining work by the **Composite Performance Index** or **Critical Ratio (CR)**, also known as the **Cost-Schedule Index (CSI)**:

$$\mathbf{CSI = CPI \times SPI}$$

The resulting EAC formula is:

$$\mathbf{EAC = AC + \frac{BAC - EV}{CPI \times SPI}}$$

#### Generalized Weighted Index Formula:
Some project governance frameworks (including NAVAIR and UK MoD) allow project managers to weight cost and schedule indices:

$$EAC = AC + \frac{BAC - EV}{w_1 \cdot CPI + w_2 \cdot SPI}$$

* Standard weights: $w_1 = 0.8$ (80% cost weight), $w_2 = 0.2$ (20% schedule weight).
* Highly schedule-critical projects (e.g., solar plant with strict tariff cutoff date): $w_1 = 0.5$, $w_2 = 0.5$.

#### When to Use:
1. **Critical Path Slippage:** When the schedule is significantly delayed ($SPI < 0.85$) and fixed overheads or contractual liquidated damages will severely inflate final costs.
2. **Worst-Case / P80 Risk Forecast:** This formula provides the most conservative (highest cost) projection, making it ideal for downside stress-testing and lender debt-service coverage ratio (DSCR) audits.

---

### 3.4 Formula 4: Bottom-Up Comprehensive Re-estimate ($EAC = AC + \text{Bottom-Up ETC}$)

#### The Method:
Rather than applying mathematical ratios to the past, the project controls team wipes out the mathematical projection and conducts a **microscopic, ground-up re-estimate** of all uncompleted work packages.
1. Inspect every uncompleted activity in the WBS.
2. Re-calculate labor hours, equipment rental days, and material quantities based on latest design drawings.
3. Obtain updated vendor quotes.
4. Sum all individual activity estimates:

$$\text{Bottom-Up } ETC = \sum_{k \in \text{Unfinished}} \text{Revised Cost to Complete}(k)$$

$$\mathbf{EAC = AC + \text{Bottom-Up } ETC}$$

#### When to Use:
1. **Baseline Invalidation:** Massive scope additions, major geological surprises (e.g., hard basalt rock discovered where soft sand was expected), or major contract renegotiations.
2. **Formal Semi-Annual Project Reviews:** High-value projects (above $100M / ₹500 Cr) typically conduct formal bottom-up EAC cycles every 6 months to validate the mathematical formulas.
3. **Accuracy:** Bottom-up re-estimates are the most accurate but also the most expensive and time-consuming to produce.

## 4. To-Complete Performance Index ($TCPI$): The Feasibility Test

### 4.1 The Core Question: "Can We Recover?"
When a project slips behind budget, executive management often issues a decree: *"We will not approve a budget increase. The project team must work smarter, cut waste, and finish within the original BAC!"*

The **To-Complete Performance Index ($TCPI$)** is the mathematical truth serum that evaluates whether such an executive demand is **feasible or delusional**.

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                 TCPI: THE MATHEMATICAL TRUTH SERUM                               │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                                                  │
│                     Work Remaining                  BAC - EV                                     │
│         TCPI = ───────────────────────── = ──────────────────────────                            │
│                     Funds Remaining              Target Budget - AC                              │
│                                                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

$TCPI$ calculates the cost efficiency that **must be achieved on all remaining work** to hit a specific financial target.

---

### 4.2 The Two Variations of TCPI

```text
┌──────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────┐
│ Target Objective     │ Formula                                         │ When to Use             │
├──────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Original Budget   │ TCPI_BAC = (BAC - EV) / (BAC - AC)              │ Management still claims │
│    (BAC)             │                                                 │ BAC is achievable.      │
├──────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤
│ 2. Revised Forecast  │ TCPI_EAC = (BAC - EV) / (EAC - AC)              │ BAC is conceded blown;  │
│    (EAC)             │                                                 │ aiming for new approved │
│                      │                                                 │ funding envelope.       │
└──────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┘
```

#### Interpretation of TCPI Values:
* **$TCPI = 1.00$:** The team must perform exactly at baseline productivity ($1.00$) for the rest of the project.
* **$TCPI < 1.00$:** The team has a cushion! They can perform with lower efficiency than baseline and still hit the target. (Occurs when the project is currently under budget, $AC < EV$).
* **$TCPI > 1.00$:** The team must perform **more efficiently** than the original plan to recover past overruns.
* **$TCPI > 1.20$:** **The Point of No Return!** Statistically unachievable in physical engineering.

---

### 4.3 The "Point of No Return" (Christensen's Empirical Law)

In the 1990s, Professor David S. Christensen of the US Air Force Institute of Technology (AFIT) published landmark empirical research analyzing cost overruns across **hundreds of major industrial and defense programs**. 

His findings established two immutable laws of project forecasting:

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                             CHRISTENSEN'S TWO EMPIRICAL LAWS OF EVM                              │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                                                  │
│  LAW 1: THE CPI STABILITY RULE (The 20% Threshold)                                               │
│  * Once a project passes 20% physical completion, cumulative CPI stabilizes.                     │
│  * Cumulative CPI rarely improves by more than 0.05 to 0.10 from its 20% milestone value!        │
│  * If CPI at 20% completion is 0.82, it will almost certainly finish between 0.77 and 0.87.      │
│                                                                                                  │
│  LAW 2: THE TCPI_BAC RECOVERY ILLUSION                                                           │
│  * If TCPI_BAC exceeds 1.20 (requiring a 20%+ efficiency gain on remaining work),                │
│    the probability of finishing within BAC is effectively ZERO (p < 0.001).                      │
│  * In 400+ analyzed programs, NOT A SINGLE PROJECT with TCPI_BAC > 1.20 ever finished within BAC!│
│                                                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                         TCPI AUDIT THRESHOLD: REALITY VS DELUSION                                │
├───────────────┬───────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ TCPI_BAC      │ Feasibility Status                │ Required Executive Action                    │
├───────────────┼───────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ ≤ 1.00        │ Readily Achievable                │ Maintain standard controls; healthy cushion. │
│ 1.01 - 1.10   │ Realistic Recovery with Effort    │ Tighten procurement, eliminate overtime.     │
│ 1.11 - 1.20   │ High Risk / Heroic Recovery       │ Formal productivity overhaul required.       │
│ > 1.20        │ STATISTICALLY IMPOSSIBLE          │ RE-BASELINE MANDATORY. Notify board/bank;   │
│               │ (The Delusion Zone)               │ de-scope deliverables or call for equity.    │
└───────────────┴───────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

> **The $\Delta(TCPI - CPI)$ Audit Trigger:**  
> A standard audit rule used by banks and senior project directors is:
> $$\Delta = TCPI_{\text{BAC}} - CPI_{\text{past}}$$
> If $\Delta > 0.10$, the schedule and cost forecast are deemed **non-credible**. A contractor operating at $CPI = 0.85$ cannot suddenly execute the remainder of the project at $TCPI = 1.15$ ($\Delta = +0.30$) without an extraordinary, proven technological breakthrough.

### 5.1 Calculation of the 4 EAC Scenarios for Surya Solar

#### Work Remaining:
$$\text{Remaining Work} = BAC - EV = ₹400\text{ Cr} - ₹180\text{ Cr} = ₹220.00\text{ Crore}$$

#### Scenario 1: Typical Past Performance ($EAC_1$)
$$EAC_1 = \frac{BAC}{CPI} = \frac{400}{0.857143} = \mathbf{₹466.67\text{ Crore}}$$
$$ETC_1 = EAC_1 - AC = ₹466.67 - ₹210.00 = \mathbf{₹256.67\text{ Crore}}$$
$$VAC_1 = BAC - EAC_1 = ₹400.00 - ₹466.67 = \mathbf{-₹66.67\text{ Crore}}$$

*Verdict:* If Rajasthan site conditions, subcontractor pace, and module delivery tariffs continue at current efficiency, the project will overrun by **₹66.67 Crore** (a 16.7% overrun).

---

#### Scenario 2: Atypical Past Variance ($EAC_2$)
$$EAC_2 = AC + (BAC - EV) = ₹210.00 + (₹400.00 - ₹180.00) = \mathbf{₹430.00\text{ Crore}}$$
$$ETC_2 = EAC_2 - AC = ₹430.00 - ₹210.00 = \mathbf{₹220.00\text{ Crore}}$$
$$VAC_2 = BAC - EAC_2 = ₹400.00 - ₹430.00 = \mathbf{-₹30.00\text{ Crore}}$$

*Verdict:* The EPC contractor argues: *"The ₹30 Crore overrun was caused by an unseasonal July Thar flash flood that washed out the access road and delayed module delivery. The road is repaired. From tomorrow, we will execute all remaining work exactly at baseline budget!"* Even under this optimistic narrative, the project finishes **₹30.00 Crore over budget**.

---

#### Scenario 3: Schedule Delay Compounding Cost ($EAC_3$)
$$EAC_3 = AC + \frac{BAC - EV}{CPI \times SPI} = 210 + \frac{220}{0.857143 \times 0.818182} = 210 + \frac{220}{0.701299} = 210 + 313.70 = \mathbf{₹523.70\text{ Crore}}$$
$$ETC_3 = EAC_3 - AC = ₹523.70 - ₹210.00 = \mathbf{₹313.70\text{ Crore}}$$
$$VAC_3 = BAC - EAC_3 = ₹400.00 - ₹523.70 = \mathbf{-₹123.70\text{ Crore}}$$

*Verdict:* This is the lender's downside risk scenario. The schedule delay pushes construction into the extreme summer heat of Rajasthan (where daytime temperatures hit 50°C, cutting labor productivity by half) and triggers contractual liquidated damages. The projected overrun explodes to **₹123.70 Crore** (30.9% overrun).

---

#### Scenario 4: Bottom-Up Re-Estimate ($EAC_4$)
During the Month 9 audit, the chief engineering team conducts a bottom-up field survey of remaining packages:
* Solar PV Module Mounting & Cabling: ₹110 Cr
* Inverter Duty Transformers & Skids: ₹45 Cr
* 220kV Switchyard & Evacuation Line: ₹55 Cr
* Commissioning, SCADA, & Punch-list Rectification: ₹35 Cr
* **Total Bottom-Up ETC:** $₹110 + ₹45 + ₹55 + ₹35 = \mathbf{₹245.00\text{ Crore}}$

$$EAC_4 = AC + \text{Bottom-Up ETC} = ₹210.00 + ₹245.00 = \mathbf{₹455.00\text{ Crore}}$$
$$VAC_4 = BAC - EAC_4 = ₹400.00 - ₹455.00 = \mathbf{-₹55.00\text{ Crore}}$$

*Verdict:* The realistic engineering forecast projects a final cost of **₹455.00 Crore** ($VAC = -₹55.00\text{ Cr}$).

### 5.3 TCPI Feasibility Evaluation for Surya Solar

Now let us evaluate whether the Surya project team can hit its targets.

#### Test 1: Can we finish within the original budget ($BAC = ₹400\text{ Cr}$)?
$$TCPI_{\text{BAC}} = \frac{BAC - EV}{BAC - AC} = \frac{400.00 - 180.00}{400.00 - 210.00} = \frac{220.00}{190.00} = \mathbf{1.1579}$$

* Analysis:
  * To finish within ₹400 Cr, the project team must achieve a $CPI$ of **1.158** for every remaining rupee of work.
  * Their historical $CPI$ to date is **0.857**.
  * Required efficiency jump: $\Delta = 1.1579 - 0.8571 = \mathbf{+0.3008}$ (a **35.1% productivity surge**).
  * Christensen Law Evaluation: In a desert environment with severe summer temperatures ahead and an existing 3-month schedule delay, achieving a 35% productivity jump is **statistically impossible**. The demand to "finish within ₹400 Cr" is a management fantasy.

#### Test 2: Can we finish within the revised board forecast ($EAC = ₹460\text{ Cr}$)?
The project sponsors negotiate with the State Bank of India consortium to increase the credit facility to ₹460 Crore ($EAC_{\text{Target}} = ₹460\text{ Cr}$).

$$TCPI_{\text{EAC}} = \frac{BAC - EV}{EAC_{\text{Target}} - AC} = \frac{400.00 - 180.00}{460.00 - 210.00} = \frac{220.00}{250.00} = \mathbf{0.8800}$$

* Analysis:
  * To finish within the revised ceiling of ₹460 Cr, the team must maintain a future $CPI$ of **0.880**.
  * Their historical $CPI$ is **0.857**.
  * Required efficiency jump: $\Delta = 0.8800 - 0.8571 = \mathbf{+0.0229}$ (a modest 2.7% improvement).
  * Christensen Law Evaluation: **Highly feasible!** With minor site rationalization, better crane scheduling, and reduced idling, moving from 0.857 to 0.880 is well within normal engineering capability.
  * Executive Action: The board approves the revised EAC of ₹460 Crore, arranges ₹60 Crore in supplemental equity and debt, and avoids contractual bankruptcy.

```go
// FeasibilityLevel defines the statistical plausibility of achieving a target TCPI.

type ForecastMethod string

const (
	MethodTypicalCPI       ForecastMethod = "TYPICAL_CPI"       // EAC = BAC / CPI
	MethodAtypical         ForecastMethod = "ATYPICAL_VARIANCE" // EAC = AC + (BAC - EV)
	MethodScheduleCostCSI  ForecastMethod = "SCHEDULE_COST_CSI" // EAC = AC + (BAC - EV)/(CPI * SPI)
```

### 6.2 Core Forecasting Calculation Engine

```go
// File: engine/evm/calculator.go
package evm

// CalculateFullForecast executes comprehensive EVM forecasting according to ANSI/EIA-748 standards.
func CalculateFullForecast(in EVMInputs) (*FullForecastReport, error) {
	if in.BAC <= 0 {
		return nil, errors.New("BAC must be strictly positive")
	}
	if in.EV < 0 || in.AC < 0 || in.PV < 0 {
		return nil, errors.New("EV, AC, and PV cannot be negative")
	}

	report := &FullForecastReport{
		BAC:             in.BAC,
		PV:              in.PV,
		EV:              in.EV,
		AC:              in.AC,
		PercentComplete: (in.EV / in.BAC) * 100.0,
	}

	// 1. Calculate Base Indices (Handling Edge Cases Safely)
	if in.AC > 0 {
		report.CPI = in.EV / in.AC
	} else if in.EV > 0 {
		report.CPI = 999.99 // Infinite efficiency (no cost recorded yet)
	} else {
		report.CPI = 1.00
	}

	if in.PV > 0 {
		report.SPI = in.EV / in.PV
	} else if in.EV > 0 {
		report.SPI = 999.99
	} else {
		report.SPI = 1.00
	}

	report.CSI = report.CPI * report.SPI

	// 2. Completed Scope Check
	remainingWork := in.BAC - in.EV
	if remainingWork <= 0.0001 {
		// Project is 100% physically complete
		completedResult := SingleEACResult{
			Method:      MethodTypicalCPI,
			EAC:         in.AC,
			ETC:         0.0,
			VAC:         in.BAC - in.AC,
			OverrunPct:  ((in.AC - in.BAC) / in.BAC) * 100.0,
			Description: "Project is physically complete. Final cost equals Actual Cost.",
		}
		report.Forecasts = append(report.Forecasts, completedResult)
		report.RecommendedEAC = in.AC
		return report, nil
	}

	// 3. Method 1: Typical Past Performance (EAC = BAC / CPI)
	var eacTypical, etcTypical float64
	if report.CPI > 0.0001 {
		eacTypical = in.BAC / report.CPI
		etcTypical = eacTypical - in.AC
	} else {
		// CPI is zero (money spent, zero work done)
		eacTypical = math.Inf(1)
		etcTypical = math.Inf(1)
	}
	report.Forecasts = append(report.Forecasts, SingleEACResult{
		Method:      MethodTypicalCPI,
		EAC:         round2(eacTypical),
		ETC:         round2(etcTypical),
		VAC:         round2(in.BAC - eacTypical),
		OverrunPct:  round2(((eacTypical - in.BAC) / in.BAC) * 100.0),
		Description: "Assumes past cost efficiency (CPI) continues across all remaining work.",
	})

	// 4. Method 2: Atypical Past Variance (EAC = AC + (BAC - EV))
	etcAtypical := remainingWork
	eacAtypical := in.AC + etcAtypical
	report.Forecasts = append(report.Forecasts, SingleEACResult{
		Method:      MethodAtypical,
		EAC:         round2(eacAtypical),
		ETC:         round2(etcAtypical),
		VAC:         round2(in.BAC - eacAtypical),
		OverrunPct:  round2(((eacAtypical - in.BAC) / in.BAC) * 100.0),
		Description: "Assumes past overruns were one-time blips; remaining work executes on budget (CPI=1.0).",
	})

	// 5. Method 3: Schedule Delay Impact (EAC = AC + (BAC - EV) / (CPI * SPI))
	var eacCSI, etcCSI float64
	if report.CSI > 0.0001 {
		etcCSI = remainingWork / report.CSI
		eacCSI = in.AC + etcCSI
	} else {
		eacCSI = math.Inf(1)
		etcCSI = math.Inf(1)
	}
	report.Forecasts = append(report.Forecasts, SingleEACResult{
		Method:      MethodScheduleCostCSI,
		EAC:         round2(eacCSI),
		ETC:         round2(etcCSI),
		VAC:         round2(in.BAC - eacCSI),
		OverrunPct:  round2(((eacCSI - in.BAC) / in.BAC) * 100.0),
		Description: "Accounts for schedule delay compounding cost via standing charges and acceleration.",
	})

	// 6. Method 4: Bottom-Up Re-estimate (if provided)
	if in.BottomUpETC != nil {
		eacBottomUp := in.AC + *in.BottomUpETC
		report.Forecasts = append(report.Forecasts, SingleEACResult{
			Method:      MethodBottomUp,
			EAC:         round2(eacBottomUp),
			ETC:         round2(*in.BottomUpETC),
			VAC:         round2(in.BAC - eacBottomUp),
			OverrunPct:  round2(((eacBottomUp - in.BAC) / in.BAC) * 100.0),
			Description: "Comprehensive engineering re-estimate of remaining work packages from first principles.",
		})
		report.RecommendedEAC = round2(eacBottomUp)
	} else {
		// Default recommended to Typical CPI
		report.RecommendedEAC = round2(eacTypical)
	}

	// 7. Calculate TCPI against BAC
	fundsRemainingBAC := in.BAC - in.AC
	report.TCPI_BAC = evaluateTCPI("BAC", in.BAC, remainingWork, fundsRemainingBAC, report.CPI)

	// 8. Calculate TCPI against Revised EAC (if provided)
	if in.TargetEAC != nil {
		fundsRemainingEAC := *in.TargetEAC - in.AC
		res := evaluateTCPI("EAC", *in.TargetEAC, remainingWork, fundsRemainingEAC, report.CPI)
		report.TCPI_EAC = &res
	}

	// 9. Christensen Rule Alert Evaluation
	if report.PercentComplete >= 20.0 && report.TCPI_BAC.TCPI > 1.20 {
		report.ChristensenAlert = true
	}

	return report, nil
}

// evaluateTCPI performs feasibility categorization and Christensen law checks.
func evaluateTCPI(targetType string, targetVal, workRemaining, fundsRemaining, pastCPI float64) TCPIResult {
	res := TCPIResult{
		TargetType:  targetType,
		TargetValue: targetVal,
	}

	if fundsRemaining <= 0 {
		res.TCPI = math.Inf(1)
		res.Feasibility = FeasibilityExhausted
		res.AuditWarning = fmt.Sprintf("CRITICAL: Funds for %s are completely exhausted! Target is impossible.", targetType)
		return res
	}

	tcpi := workRemaining / fundsRemaining
	res.TCPI = round4(tcpi)
	res.DeltaVsPastCPI = round4(tcpi - pastCPI)

	switch {
	case tcpi <= 1.00:
		res.Feasibility = FeasibilityReadilyAchievable
		res.AuditWarning = "Healthy cost cushion. Target is readily achievable."
	case tcpi <= 1.10:
		res.Feasibility = FeasibilityRealistic
		res.AuditWarning = "Target achievable with tight site supervision and waste reduction."
	case tcpi <= 1.20:
		res.Feasibility = FeasibilityHighRisk
		res.AuditWarning = "HIGH RISK: Requires heroic recovery. Highly vulnerable to future delay."
	default:
		res.Feasibility = FeasibilityImpossible
		res.AuditWarning = "STATISTICALLY UNFEASIBLE (Point of No Return). Immediate re-baseline mandatory."
	}

	return res
}

func round2(val float64) float64 {
	return math.Round(val*100) / 100
}

func round4(val float64) float64 {
	return math.Round(val*10000) / 10000
}
```

```tsx
              <p className="text-xs text-slate-400 mt-2">{tcpiEac.auditWarning}</p>
            </div>
          )}
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  );
};
```

## 8. Summary Checklist for Senior Project Controls Engineers

Before claiming mastery of Earned Value Forecasting, verify that you can answer and defend each of the following:

1. **The Formula Choice Principle:** Why is applying $EAC = \frac{BAC}{CPI}$ hazardous when past overruns were caused by a one-off insured flood event?
2. **The Schedule Penalty:** Why does $EAC = AC + \frac{BAC - EV}{CPI \times SPI}$ produce the highest projected overrun, and what real-world costs does the $SPI$ denominator account for?
3. **The P6 Sign Convention:** Why is a negative $VAC$ ($-₹66.67\text{ Cr}$) an unfavorable cost overrun, while a positive $VAC$ is favorable?
4. **The Sunk Cost Axiom:** Why is $AC$ present in every valid formulation of $EAC$ except the algebraic shortcut $BAC / CPI$?
5. **Christensen's 20% Stability Law:** If your project is 35% complete with $CPI = 0.81$ and the project manager promises to finish at $CPI = 1.05$, why should the lender reject the report?
6. **TCPI Denominator Exhaustion:** What does it mean mathematically and commercially when $BAC - AC \le 0$, and what value does $TCPI$ yield?
