## 1. Executive Summary & The Core Mental Model

### 1.1 Why Projects Never Spend Money Linearly
In amateur financial models, accountants often distribute project budgets with a ruler: a ₹360 Crore project scheduled for 12 months is assumed to spend ₹30 Crore every month ($₹360 / 12$).

In physical construction, civil infrastructure, and industrial engineering, **linear expenditure is a physical and organizational impossibility**. 

A project cannot spend ₹30 Crore in Month 1 because:
* Land access permits are still pending;
* Subcontractors have not mobilized their site offices;
* Detailed engineering drawings are still being stamped;
* Heavy equipment is in transit across the ocean.

Similarly, a project cannot spend ₹30 Crore in Month 12 because:
* Physical erection is already complete;
* Only a handful of commissioning engineers and testing technicians remain on site checking SCADA wiring and loop tests.

Instead, every physical project begins with a gradual ramp-up, accelerates into a ferocious peak production phase, and tapers off into a gradual commissioning plateau. When you plot cumulative expenditure over time, the result is invariably a **sigmoidal "S-Curve"**.

### 1.2 The Plain-English Analogy: The Long-Haul Flight Profile
To understand the physics of the S-Curve before delving into time-phased bucket aggregation algorithms, consider a commercial Boeing 777 passenger flight from New Delhi to London:

```text

│                            THE BOEING 777 FLIGHT PROFILE ANALOGY                                 │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                                                  │

│     * Cruising at Mach 0.84 (900 km/h). Engines burn fuel at maximum sustained velocity.         │
│     * Distance accumulated per hour is at its highest, steady-state rate.                        │
│     * The cumulative S-Curve forms its steepest, nearly linear diagonal slope.                   │
│     * Analogy: Mass pile-driving, concrete pouring, solar panel mounting, cable pulling.         │
│                                                                                                  │
│  3. Descent, Approach, and Gate Taxi (Phase 3: Commissioning & Plateau):                         │
│     * Throttles pulled back to idle, flaps extended, slowing to 140 knots for touchdown.         │
```

The derivative of the cumulative S-Curve ($\frac{d}{dt}\text{Cumulative Spend}$) is the **Periodic Cash Flow Histogram** (the burn rate per month). A project's financial risk profile is defined not merely by *how much* it spends, but by *when* that cash must be drawn down.

## 3. The "Banana Curve" Envelope (Early Dates vs Late Dates)

### 3.1 What Generates the Envelope?
In Module 4, we learned that every activity in a Critical Path Method (CPM) schedule possesses two sets of valid boundary coordinates:
1. **Early Dates ($ES, EF$):** The earliest possible time the activity can execute based on predecessor logic.
2. **Late Dates ($LS, LF$):** The latest possible time the activity can execute without delaying the project's contractual completion milestone.

When you spread an activity's budget across its **Early Dates** and sum across all project activities for each calendar month, you generate the **Cumulative Early Planned Value ($PV_{\text{Early}}$)** curve.

When you spread the identical activity budgets across their **Late Dates** and sum across all calendar months, you generate the **Cumulative Late Planned Value ($PV_{\text{Late}}$)** curve.

Because $ES \le LS$ and $EF \le LF$ for every activity, the Early Curve always sits above or coincident with the Late Curve. The space enclosed between them resembles a banana, giving rise to the universal industry term: **The Banana Curve**.

## 4. Time-Phased Cost Distribution (Spreading Curves)

### 4.1 The Database Problem
In the project database, an activity record looks like this:
```text
Activity ID: ACT-CIV-201
Name: Construct Main Inverter Foundations
Duration: 40 working days (01-Feb-2026 to 25-Mar-2026)
Budget (BAC): ₹4,000,000
```
When generating a financial forecast for February vs March, **how is that ₹4,000,000 allocated across the individual working days?**

This is determined by the activity's **Resource Distribution Curve** (in Primavera P6, stored in table `CURVE`).

---

### 4.2 The 4 Canonical Distribution Spreading Functions

```text
┌─────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Distribution    │ Daily Cost Formula c(t), t ∈ [0, D]      │ Real-World Application              │
├─────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Linear       │ c(t) = BAC / D                           │ Standard ongoing labor, security,   │
│    (Uniform)    │ (Constant daily expenditure)             │ site scaffolding leases.            │
├─────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. Front-Loaded │ c(t) = (2 * BAC / D) * (1 - t / D)       │ Major equipment procurement with    │
│    (Decreasing) │ (High start, tapering to zero)           │ 80% advance payment; mobilization.  │
├─────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. Back-Loaded  │ c(t) = (2 * BAC / D) * (t / D)           │ Testing, commissioning, system      │
│    (Increasing) │ (Starts near zero, peaks at completion)  │ punch-list rectification, handover. │
├─────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 4. Bell-Curve   │ c(t) ∝ sin²(π * t / D) or Gaussian       │ Major civil works, structural       │
│    (Trapezoid)  │ (Gradual start, peak middle, slow finish)│ erection, cable pulling (crew ramp).│
└─────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 4.3 P6 Standard 21-Point Distribution Curves
In Oracle Primavera P6, custom curves are modeled as **21-point percentage distribution arrays** (every 5% of elapsed activity duration from 0% to 100%):

$$\sum_{k=0}^{20} p_k = 100.0\%$$

When calculating the budget allocated to Day $d$ of an activity of duration $D$:
1. Calculate elapsed fraction: $f = d / D$.
2. Interpolate between interval points $k$ and $k+1$.
3. Multiply the interpolated percentage by total activity budget $BAC$.

## 5. Monthly Billing Schedules & Bank Syndicate Project Finance

### 5.1 The Project Finance Capital Stack
In large infrastructure projects (such as the Surya 100MW Solar IPP Project), funding is structured through **Non-Recourse Project Finance**:
* **Debt Ratio (70%):** Provided by a banking consortium (e.g., State Bank of India, IREDA) at floating interest rates.
* **Equity Ratio (30%):** Injected by the project developer/sponsors.

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                             PROJECT FINANCE CAPITAL WATERFALL                                    │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                                                  │
│  TOTAL PROJECT CAPITAL COST: ₹400.00 Crore                                                       │
│  ├── Sponsor Equity (30%): ₹120.00 Crore  (Injected FIRST during early civil works)              │
│  └── Bank Debt (70%):      ₹280.00 Crore  (Drawn down in monthly tranches against verified IPCs) │
│                                                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

### 5.2 Monthly Billing & Interim Payment Certificates (IPCs)
Under standard FIDIC EPC contracts, money is disbursed through **Interim Payment Certificates (IPCs)**:

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                           MONTHLY CONTRACTOR IPC BILLING FORMULA                                 │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                                                  │
│   Net Monthly Cash Disbursement = Gross Certified Earned Value (EV_month)                        │
│                                 - Retention Money Deduction (5% to 10%)                          │
│                                 - Mobilization Advance Amortization (typically 10% to 15%)       │
│                                 - Liquidated Damages / Penalties (if any)                         │
│                                 + Approved Variation Claims                                      │
│                                                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

* **Retention Money (5%):** Held by the owner in an escrow account until the expiration of the 12-month Defects Liability Period (DLP) after commercial operation.
* **Mobilization Advance:** An upfront loan (typically 10% of contract value) provided to the contractor to mobilize plant and equipment, recovered proportionally from monthly invoices once progress exceeds 20%.

---

### 5.3 The Impact of Interest During Construction ($IDC$)
When debt is drawn early (following the $PV_{\text{Early}}$ curve), the borrower pays compounding interest during the 18-month construction period before the solar plant earns its first rupee of revenue.

$$IDC = \sum_{m=1}^{N} \text{Cumulative Debt Drawn}(m) \times \left(\frac{r_{\text{annual}}}{12}\right)$$

* At an interest rate of $9.5\%$ p.a., executing on the extreme Early curve versus a balanced middle curve can inflate total IDC by **₹8.0 to ₹14.0 Crore**!
* Conversely, postponing work towards the Late curve saves IDC, but introduces existential risk: any delay causes the developer to breach power purchase agreement (PPA) deadlines, risking cancellation of the solar feed-in tariff.

## 6. Integration with ERP Financial Ledgers (SAP PS / Oracle Financials)

A modern project controls engine must synchronize its time-phased schedule with enterprise financial systems (e.g., **SAP Project System (PS)** or **Oracle ERP Cloud**).

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                              THE 5-STAGE COMMITMENT LIFECYCLE                                    │
├───────────┬─────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Stage     │ Financial Ledger State              │ P6 / Scheduling Engine Counterpart             │
├───────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Budget │ Cost Baseline / WBS Allocation      │ Planned Value (BAC)                            │
│ 2. PR     │ Purchase Requisition (Internal)     │ Uncommitted Resource Requirement               │
│ 3. PO     │ Purchase Order (Legally Committed)  │ Committed Cost (Future AC Liability)           │
│ 4. GRN    │ Goods Receipt Note / Physical Site  │ Incurred / Accrued Actual Cost (AC)            │
│ 5. Paid   │ Vendor Invoice Cleared in Treasury  │ Disbursed Cash Flow (Treasury Outflow)         │
└───────────┴─────────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

> **The Developer's Golden Rule of EVM Accounting:**  
> When computing **Actual Cost ($AC$)** for Earned Value analysis, **always use Stage 4 (Goods Receipt / Physical Work Incurred)**, NOT Stage 5 (Invoices Paid).  
> If a vendor installs ₹20 Crore of transformers in September, but the corporate finance department takes 90 days to process the paper invoice, the work has been incurred physically. If you wait for the cash payment, your $AC$ will be falsely deflated, creating a temporary, illusory $CPI > 1.0$ that collapses into a catastrophic overrun three months later.

#### Diagnostic Evaluation:
1. **Schedule Status:** $EV$ (₹200 Cr) is above $PV_{\text{Late}}$ (₹164 Cr). The project has **not yet breached the negative float boundary**. Total float has been consumed from the ideal early schedule, but the critical path is still within contractual bounds.
2. **Cost Status:** $AC$ (₹240 Cr) is tracking right near the $PV_{\text{Early}}$ ceiling (₹244 Cr). The contractor is **spending cash at the Early rate**, but delivering progress only slightly above the Late rate.
3. **Cumulative Cost Variance:** $CV = EV - AC = ₹200 - ₹240 = \mathbf{-₹40.00\text{ Crore}}$.
4. **Cumulative CPI:** $CPI = 200 / 240 = \mathbf{0.8333}$.
5. **Financing Warning:** Because cash spend is running at the Early rate, debt drawdowns are accelerated, driving up Interest During Construction while physical output lags.

## 8. Software Architecture: Golang Time-Phased Bucket Aggregation Engine

In a high-performance P6 scheduling engine, time-phasing cannot be calculated by querying SQL rows for every day. We implement a **high-throughput memory bucket aggregation engine** in Go.

```go
// Package cashflow provides high-performance time-phased S-Curve generation.
// File: engine/cashflow/engine.go
package cashflow

import (
	"errors"
	"math"
	"time"
)

// DistributionType defines the mathematical curve used to spread activity costs.
type DistributionType string

const (
	DistLinear      DistributionType = "LINEAR"
	DistFrontLoaded DistributionType = "FRONT_LOADED"
	DistBackLoaded  DistributionType = "BACK_LOADED"
	DistBellCurve   DistributionType = "BELL_CURVE"
)

// ActivityScheduleInput represents an activity with early/late dates and budget.
type ActivityScheduleInput struct {
	ID           string
	Name         string
	BAC          float64
	Distribution DistributionType
	EarlyStart   time.Time
	EarlyFinish  time.Time
	LateStart    time.Time
	LateFinish   time.Time
}

// MonthlyBucket represents a calendar month's aggregated financial figures.
type MonthlyBucket struct {
	YearMonth        string  `json:"year_month"` // e.g. "2026-05"
	EarlyPeriodic    float64 `json:"early_periodic"`
	EarlyCumulative  float64 `json:"early_cumulative"`
	LatePeriodic     float64 `json:"late_periodic"`
	LateCumulative   float64 `json:"late_cumulative"`
	ActualPeriodic   float64 `json:"actual_periodic"`
	ActualCumulative float64 `json:"actual_cumulative"`
	EarnedPeriodic   float64 `json:"earned_periodic"`
	EarnedCumulative float64 `json:"earned_cumulative"`
}

// SCurveEnvelopeResult represents the complete multi-line S-Curve dataset.
type SCurveEnvelopeResult struct {
	ProjectBAC float64         `json:"project_bac"`
	Buckets    []MonthlyBucket `json:"buckets"`
}

// GenerateSCurveEnvelope aggregates activity cash flows into monthly buckets.
func GenerateSCurveEnvelope(
	activities []ActivityScheduleInput,
	projectStart, projectEnd time.Time,
) (*SCurveEnvelopeResult, error) {
	if projectEnd.Before(projectStart) {
		return nil, errors.New("projectEnd must be after projectStart")
	}

	// 1. Initialize Monthly Bucket Maps
	bucketKeys := generateMonthKeys(projectStart, projectEnd)
	earlyMap := make(map[string]float64)
	lateMap := make(map[string]float64)

	var totalBAC float64

	// 2. Spread Activity Costs
	for _, act := range activities {
		totalBAC += act.BAC

		// Spread Early
		spreadActivity(act.BAC, act.EarlyStart, act.EarlyFinish, act.Distribution, earlyMap)

		// Spread Late
		spreadActivity(act.BAC, act.LateStart, act.LateFinish, act.Distribution, lateMap)
	}

	// 3. Assemble Buckets with Running Cumulative Totals
	var res SCurveEnvelopeResult
	res.ProjectBAC = math.Round(totalBAC*100) / 100

	var cumEarly, cumLate float64

	for _, k := range bucketKeys {
		earlyPer := earlyMap[k]
		latePer := lateMap[k]

		cumEarly += earlyPer
		cumLate += latePer

		b := MonthlyBucket{
			YearMonth:       k,
			EarlyPeriodic:   math.Round(earlyPer*100) / 100,
			EarlyCumulative: math.Round(cumEarly*100) / 100,
			LatePeriodic:    math.Round(latePer*100) / 100,
			LateCumulative:  math.Round(cumLate*100) / 100,
		}
		res.Buckets = append(res.Buckets, b)
	}

	return &res, nil
}

// spreadActivity distributes BAC across working days into monthly bucket map.
func spreadActivity(
	bac float64,
	start, finish time.Time,
	dist DistributionType,
	dest map[string]float64,
) {
	if finish.Before(start) || bac <= 0 {
		return
	}

	totalDays := int(finish.Sub(start).Hours()/24) + 1
	if totalDays <= 0 {
		totalDays = 1
	}

	for d := 0; d < totalDays; d++ {
		currentDay := start.AddDate(0, 0, d)
		monthKey := currentDay.Format("2006-01")

		// Calculate normalized daily weight w(t), where sum(w) = 1.0
		weight := calculateDayWeight(d, totalDays, dist)
		dailyCost := bac * weight

		dest[monthKey] += dailyCost
	}
}

// calculateDayWeight computes the relative weight for day index d.
func calculateDayWeight(d, totalDays int, dist DistributionType) float64 {
	N := float64(totalDays)
	if N <= 1 {
		return 1.0
	}

	t := float64(d) + 0.5 // Mid-day sample

	switch dist {
	case DistFrontLoaded:
		// Linear decreasing: w(t) = (2 / N) * (1 - t / N)
		return (2.0 / N) * (1.0 - (t / N)) / float64(totalDays) * N

	case DistBackLoaded:
		// Linear increasing: w(t) = (2 / N) * (t / N)
		return (2.0 / N) * (t / N) / float64(totalDays) * N

	case DistBellCurve:
		// Sine-squared approximation: sin^2(pi * t / N)
		sinVal := math.Sin((math.Pi * t) / N)
		rawWeight := sinVal * sinVal
		// Normalized approximation factor
		return rawWeight / (N * 0.5)

	case DistLinear:
		fallthrough
	default:
		return 1.0 / N
	}
}

// generateMonthKeys creates an ordered slice of "YYYY-MM" strings.
func generateMonthKeys(start, end time.Time) []string {
	var keys []string
	cur := time.Date(start.Year(), start.Month(), 1, 0, 0, 0, 0, time.UTC)
	last := time.Date(end.Year(), end.Month(), 1, 0, 0, 0, 0, time.UTC)

	for !cur.After(last) {
		keys = append(keys, cur.Format("2006-01"))
		cur = cur.AddDate(0, 1, 0)
	}
	return keys
}
```

## 9. Frontend Architecture: Next.js + SVG Interactive S-Curve Component

In enterprise web applications, rendering multi-line S-Curves with responsive SVG vector graphics ensures clean visualization across monitors, tablets, and mobile screens.

```tsx
// components/cashflow/BananaSCurveChart.tsx
'use client';

import React, { useState } from 'react';

export interface MonthBucket {
  year_month: string;
  early_periodic: number;
  early_cumulative: number;
  late_periodic: number;
  late_cumulative: number;
  actual_cumulative?: number;
  earned_cumulative?: number;
}

interface BananaSCurveProps {
  bac: number;
  buckets: MonthBucket[];
}

export const BananaSCurveChart: React.FC<BananaSCurveProps> = ({ bac, buckets }) => {
  const [hoverIndex, setHoverIndex] = useState<number | null>(null);

  const width = 800;
  const height = 400;
  const padding = { top: 30, right: 40, bottom: 50, left: 60 };

  const chartWidth = width - padding.left - padding.right;
  const chartHeight = height - padding.top - padding.bottom;

  const n = buckets.length;
  const getX = (idx: number) => padding.left + (idx / (n - 1)) * chartWidth;
  const getY = (val: number) => padding.top + chartHeight - (val / bac) * chartHeight;

  // Generate SVG Path String for Cumulative Line
  const generateLinePath = (accessor: (b: MonthBucket) => number) => {
    return buckets
      .map((b, i) => `${i === 0 ? 'M' : 'L'} ${getX(i)} ${getY(accessor(b))}`)
      .join(' ');
  };

  // Generate Closed Polygon Path for Banana Area (Early -> Late -> Close)
  const generateBananaArea = () => {
    const earlyPoints = buckets.map((b, i) => `${getX(i)},${getY(b.early_cumulative)}`).join(' ');
    const latePoints = [...buckets]
      .reverse()
      .map((b, i) => `${getX(n - 1 - i)},${getY(b.late_cumulative)}`)
      .join(' ');
    return `M ${earlyPoints} L ${latePoints} Z`;
  };

  const hoveredBucket = hoverIndex !== null ? buckets[hoverIndex] : null;

  return (
    <div className="bg-slate-900 border border-slate-800 rounded-xl p-6 shadow-2xl text-slate-100 max-w-5xl mx-auto">
      <div className="flex flex-col sm:flex-row sm:items-center justify-between pb-4 border-b border-slate-800 gap-2">
        <div>
          <h2 className="text-lg font-bold tracking-tight text-white flex items-center gap-2">
            📈 Cumulative S-Curve & Banana Envelope
          </h2>
          <p className="text-xs text-slate-400">Total Budget (BAC): ₹{bac.toFixed(1)} Crore</p>
        </div>
        <div className="flex items-center gap-4 text-xs font-medium">
          <span className="flex items-center gap-1.5">
            <span className="w-3 h-0.5 bg-blue-400 inline-block" /> PV Early
          </span>
          <span className="flex items-center gap-1.5">
            <span className="w-3 h-0.5 bg-amber-400 inline-block" /> PV Late
          </span>
          <span className="flex items-center gap-1.5">
            <span className="w-3 h-0.5 bg-rose-400 inline-block" /> Actual Cost (AC)
          </span>
          <span className="flex items-center gap-1.5">
            <span className="w-3 h-0.5 bg-emerald-400 inline-block" /> Earned Value (EV)
          </span>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div className="relative mt-4">
        <svg viewBox={`0 0 ${width} ${height}`} className="w-full h-auto">
          {/* Grid lines */}
          {[0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0].map((pct) => (
            <g key={pct}>
              <line
                x1={padding.left}
                y1={getY(bac * pct)}
                x2={width - padding.right}
                y2={getY(bac * pct)}
                stroke="#334155"
                strokeDasharray="4 4"
                strokeWidth="1"
              />
              <text x={padding.left - 10} y={getY(bac * pct) + 4} textAnchor="end" className="text-[10px] fill-slate-400">
                ₹{(bac * pct).toFixed(0)}Cr
              </text>
            </g>
          ))}

          {/* Banana Envelope Area */}
          <path d={generateBananaArea()} fill="rgba(59, 130, 246, 0.12)" stroke="none" />

          {/* PV Late Curve */}
          <path d={generateLinePath((b) => b.late_cumulative)} fill="none" stroke="#fbbf24" strokeWidth="2.5" />

          {/* PV Early Curve */}
          <path d={generateLinePath((b) => b.early_cumulative)} fill="none" stroke="#60a5fa" strokeWidth="2.5" />

          {/* Actual Cost Line (where recorded) */}
          <path
            d={generateLinePath((b) => b.actual_cumulative ?? 0)}
            fill="none"
            stroke="#f43f5e"
            strokeWidth="3"
            strokeDasharray="2 0"
          />

          {/* Earned Value Line (where recorded) */}

            d={generateLinePath((b) => b.earned_cumulative ?? 0)}
            fill="none"

            strokeWidth="3"

          {buckets.map((b, i) => (
            <text key={b.year_month} x={getX(i)} y={height - padding.bottom + 20} textAnchor="middle" className="text-[10px] fill-slate-400">

          {/* Interactive Mouse Hover Crosshair */}
          {hoverIndex !== null && (

              x1={getX(hoverIndex)}
              y1={padding.top}
              x2={getX(hoverIndex)}
              y2={height - padding.bottom}
              stroke="#cbd5e1"
              strokeWidth="1"
              strokeDasharray="2 2"
            />
          )}

          {/* Invisible Overlay Rectangles for Hover Detection */}
          {buckets.map((b, i) => (
            <rect
              key={`hover-${b.year_month}`}
              x={getX(i) - chartWidth / (n * 2)}
              y={padding.top}
              width={chartWidth / n}
              height={chartHeight}
              fill="transparent"
              className="cursor-pointer"
              onMouseEnter={() => setHoverIndex(i)}
              onMouseLeave={() => setHoverIndex(null)}
            />
          ))}
        </svg>

        {/* Hover Tooltip Card */}
        {hoveredBucket && (
          <div className="mt-3 p-3 rounded-lg bg-slate-950 border border-slate-800 text-xs flex flex-wrap gap-4 justify-between font-mono">
            <div>
              <span className="text-slate-400">Month:</span> <strong className="text-white">{hoveredBucket.year_month}</strong>

            <div>
              <span className="text-blue-400">Early PV:</span> ₹{hoveredBucket.early_cumulative.toFixed(1)} Cr
            </div>
            <div>
              <span className="text-amber-400">Late PV:</span> ₹{hoveredBucket.late_cumulative.toFixed(1)} Cr

              </div>
            )}
          </div>
        )}
      </div>
    </div>
  );
};
```

## 10. Summary Checklist for Senior Project Controls Engineers

Before signing off on cash flows and S-Curves, verify your ability to answer and defend:

1. **The Banana Envelope Boundary:** Why does physical progress ($EV$) dropping below the $PV_{\text{Late}}$ curve prove mathematically that project Total Float is negative?
2. **The Financing Paradox:** Why is completing every task on its earliest date ($ES$) bad for project finance debt service costs (IDC)?
3. **The Spreading Function Choice:** When should a front-loaded curve be assigned rather than a linear or bell-curve distribution?
4. **The Inflection Point Risk:** Why do construction contractors experience their highest rate of liquidity distress at the S-Curve inflection point?
5. **Accrual vs Cash Outflow:** Why does EVM mandate measuring $AC$ at the Goods Receipt / Service Incurred stage rather than Treasury cash payment?
