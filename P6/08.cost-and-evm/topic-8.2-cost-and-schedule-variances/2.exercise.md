## 🧠 Section A: Conceptual & Architectural Principles

### Question 1: The Directionality & Sign Convention of EVM Variances
In traditional financial accounting, an accountant calculates variance as $\text{Budget} - \text{Actual}$. In ANSI/EIA-748 Earned Value Management, Cost Variance is strictly defined as $EV - AC$, and Schedule Variance as $EV - PV$.
1. Explain why EVM places $EV$ first in both formulas ($EV - AC$ and $EV - PV$).
2. Under what operational conditions will $CV$ be:
   - Greater than 0?
   - Exactly equal to 0?
   - Less than 0?
3. If an engineer claims that their project has an $SV$ of -₹10 Crore, what does the negative sign signify in terms of contractual deliverables?

### Question 2: The Denominator Dilemma in Variance Percentages
Consider the two variance percentage formulas:
$$CV\% = \left( \frac{EV - AC}{EV} \right) \times 100 \qquad \text{and} \qquad SV\% = \left( \frac{EV - PV}{PV} \right) \times 100$$
1. Why does $CV\%$ divide by $EV$ rather than $BAC$ or $AC$? Provide a concrete example showing how dividing by $BAC$ conceals a catastrophic cost overrun in the early stages of a project.
2. What edge-case error occurs in the $CV\%$ formula when an activity has incurred actual costs ($AC > 0$) but zero physical progress has been achieved ($EV = 0$)? How should a backend Go engine handle this division-by-zero condition?
3. Why does $SV\%$ divide by $PV$ rather than $BAC$?

### Question 3: The Schedule Variance in Dollars Paradox
The EPC Contract for the Surya 100MW Solar plant has a total budget ($BAC$) of ₹400 Crore and a scheduled completion date of Month 12. 
Due to severe delays in obtaining grid synchronisation clearances from the state transmission utility, the contractor finishes all physical work at **Month 20** (8 months late).
1. Calculate the theoretical values of $PV$, $EV$, and $SV$ at Month 20 upon final commissioning.
2. Explain the fundamental paradox: Why does traditional Schedule Variance ($SV$) report **₹0.00** for a project that finished 8 months late?
3. Sketch an ASCII graph demonstrating the trajectory of $SV$ over time from Month 12 to Month 20. Why is this trajectory dangerously deceptive when presented to bank lenders and executive sponsors?

In 2003, Walt Lipke introduced the concept of **Earned Schedule ($ES$)**.
