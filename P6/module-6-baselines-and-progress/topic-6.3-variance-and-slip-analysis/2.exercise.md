## 🧠 Section A: Mathematical Foundations & Sign Conventions

### Question 1: The P6 Sign Convention Proof
In Oracle Primavera P6, Finish Variance is defined as:
$$SV_{\text{finish}} = \text{Baseline Finish} - \text{Early Finish}$$
A junior software developer questions this formula:
*"In normal math and Git diffs, drift is New - Old. Why didn't P6 define variance as $\text{Early Finish} - \text{Baseline Finish}$?"*
1. Provide the mathematical, psychological, and Earned Value Management ($EVM$) justification for why P6 defines variance as $\text{Baseline} - \text{Current}$.
2. Under what conditions is $SV_{\text{finish}} > 0$? Under what conditions is $SV_{\text{finish}} < 0$?
3. If an activity had a Baseline Finish of `2026-06-15` and finishes on `2026-06-25`, what is the calculated variance? State clearly whether this represents a favorable or unfavorable outcome.

## 🏗️ Section B: Float Erosion & Critical Path Migration

### Question 4: The "Float Consumption" Trap
Consider Activity $A$ in a construction project:
* Original Baseline Finish: `2026-04-10`
* Original Total Float: `+25 working days`
* Due to equipment delivery delays, Activity $A$'s Early Finish slips to `2026-04-24` (10 working days of delay).
* The Project Milestone (COD) finish date remains completely unchanged.
1. What is the Finish Variance of Activity $A$?
2. What is the new Total Float of Activity $A$?
3. If the Project Director presents a monthly executive dashboard stating: *"The project is 100% on schedule because our completion date has not moved,"* why is this statement dangerously misleading?
4. What catastrophic risk does float consumption impose on downstream activities that share the same CPM path?

* **Path 2 (220kV Underground Cabling):** Early Finish = `2026-08-30`, Total Float = `0 days` (Original Critical Path).
1. The 220kV cabling on Path 2 finishes on time. However, severe monsoon rains delay the Civil Switchyard on Path 1 by 25 days.
2. Trace what happens to:
   - The Early Finish of Path 1.
   - The Total Float of Path 1.
   - The Total Float of Path 2.
