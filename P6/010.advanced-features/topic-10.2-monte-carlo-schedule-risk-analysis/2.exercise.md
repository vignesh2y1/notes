### Question 1: Mathematical Proof of Merge Event Bias
Consider two parallel activities, Path 1 and Path 2, merging into milestone $M$. Their durations $X_1$ and $X_2$ are independent random variables uniformly distributed:
$$X_1 \sim \text{Uniform}(10, 20), \quad X_2 \sim \text{Uniform}(10, 20)$$
1. What is the deterministic CPM duration of milestone $M$ using expected values $\mathbb{E}[X_1]$ and $\mathbb{E}[X_2]$?
2. Derive the exact Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) and Probability Density Function (PDF) of $T = \max(X_1, X_2)$.
3. Calculate the true mathematical expectation $\mathbb{E}[T]$. What is the percentage error introduced by the deterministic CPM approach?
4. How does the expected value change if three identical independent paths merge ($T_3 = \max(X_1, X_2, X_3)$)?

Deterministic CPM schedules both branches at their Most Likely durations ($140$d and $145$d), yielding a deterministic COD of Day 145 (giving an apparent 35-day safety margin against the 180-day deadline).
1. Explain qualitatively why the actual probability of completing before Day 180 is significantly lower than suggested by the deterministic buffer.
2. If a 10,000-iteration Monte Carlo simulation reveals $P_{50} = 156$ days and $P_{80} = 178$ days, what schedule contingency buffer should the EPC director add to the baseline commitment?
